Word order: the three-word lock

All playable — tap the words. The first three lock together, the moment all three are in the right order. Nothing before that, so there is no per-tap signal to probe.

5 · The first three lock as a bundleyour call

Get the opening three into the right order and they snap shut together, filling solid. Until then nothing happens — so a single tap tells you nothing, and there is no way to feel out one word at a time. After the lock the rest is yours to build, with the start settled under you.

The same rule on a short sentencedecided: flat three

Five words, three locked in one go — it does hand over most of a short sentence. Decided anyway: a flat three, whatever the length. One rule the learner can hold in their head beats a formula that quietly changes behaviour with sentence length, and bundling already stops it being exploitable. A short sentence is an easy sentence; it is not the one worth protecting.

Where this landed

Bundling is what closes the exploit. Locking word-by-word answers a question on every tap — is this one right? — which is exactly what makes guessing pay. Locking only when all three are correct answers nothing until you have committed to an order, so there is no cheap probe. Combined with the cap of three, guessing stops being a winning strategy.

Two things I'd still settle before building:

  • Three, or half the sentence? Decided: flat three. A single rule the learner can predict is worth more than a formula that changes with sentence length.
  • Does the lock affect grading? Left as is — there is no grade on this exercise yet. Revisit if one arrives.
